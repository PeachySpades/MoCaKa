# MoCaKa

## Echo Caves

Moka the bat is lost in pitch-black caves. Squeak to send out a ring of sound that lights up the walls for a moment, but every squeak also wakes whatever is sleeping nearby: spiders drop on their threads, loose rocks fall, and owls give chase.

Echoes are limited. Each cave gives Moka a set number of squeaks, and glowing crystals add 3 more. When you run out, you fly blind.

### Modes

- **Explore**: three long hand-made caves (First Flight, The Hollows, Owl Deep), each harder than the last. Collect the glowing moths and fly to the green light. You earn one star per cave for escaping, one for finding every moth, and one for finishing with enough echoes left. Play picks up at the next cave you haven't beaten. Each cave has a lantern checkpoint (cave 3 has two): light it by flying past, and if you lose after that, "Back to checkpoint" puts you there with full hearts and at least 6 echoes. Two extra hearts are hidden in hard-to-reach spots in every cave, each adding a heart (up to 5). A **minimap** in the bottom-right corner (under DASH) shows the explored cave around Moka as you fly; tap it (or press M or Tab) to open the big map, an old-school dungeon map of the cave, but only the parts you've seen, by flying past or lighting them with echoes: Moka blinks on it, with the lanterns (lit or not), the exit and any moths and hearts you've spotted. The game waits while the map is open; tap anywhere, M or Escape to close it. The map starts blank in each new cave and is kept when you go back to a checkpoint.
- **Cave Run**: an endless side-scroller. The screen scrolls right on its own and speeds up over time. If a wall pins Moka against the left edge, the dark catches you. Each run is a newly generated tunnel, and your best distance is saved. Explore and Cave Run both have the same dash as Battle (DASH button, a quick flick, or G/Shift).
- **Fighting back (Explore and Cave Run)**: echoes only light the cave and wake things; they never stun. The dash is a dive bite: a big chomp knocks out any spider or owl it touches without hurting Moka. The BITE button (next to DASH, or H/X) snaps the big battle chomp in front of Moka, with ragged claw marks, and hits everything in that arc: monsters, spider threads and flying crystal pieces. Knocked-out monsters tumble to the floor, lie dizzy and fade away.
- **Danger overhead**: some ceiling crystals are loose (a careful eye spots a glinting crack when they're lit). An echo cracks one and it bursts into sharp flying pieces that cost a heart, so dodge or slash them. Dashing or slashing through a spider's thread drops the spider to the floor, knocked out; it only hurts if it lands on you.
- **Multiplayer**: the title's Multiplayer card opens the lobby. Two tabs at the top pick the game: **Battle** or **Co-op Run**. The seat cards show you, friends, CPU bats (each with its own Easy/Normal/Hard setting) and empty seats to add a CPU or invite a friend, with a **Customize your bat** button under them. A full four-bat battle shows a **+ Big Brawl** bar: Battle takes up to 8 bats, and past four the seats become two rows of four inside a spinning rainbow frame, seats 5 to 8 get their own colours (cyan, red, yellow and pearl), and the Battle tab turns into a pulsing **Big Brawl** tab. Co-op Run stays at up to 4 bats (switching to it lets extra CPU bats go, and it can't be picked while more than four people are in the room). The **Rules** panel on the right fits on one screen: for Battle, **Free-for-all** or **Rounds**, the arena, bites or rounds to win, power-ups and the view; for Co-op Run, the goal, map, monsters and view. Along the bottom are the lobby's own music button, the online row and the Let's Fight button.
- **Bat creator**: "Customize your bat" opens the creator: category tabs (Body, Wings, Face, Hats) on the left and your 3D bat on a stone pedestal in the middle. Slide through the options with the big arrows or a flick on the bat (a slow drag spins it); the name and a counter show where you are. Parts: bat type, colours, wings, eyes, face extra, ears and hat. Your bat is saved on the device and used in every mode, and friends online see it too. Bats have no floating name tags in any mode; the HUD at the top shows who is who.
- **Battle (Bat Brawl)**: 2 to 8 bats in a pitch-black arena. You can't see walls, rivals or pickups until sound lights them up, and squeaking also gives away where you are. Dash into a rival to bite them (a big snapping mouth, slash marks and a crunch). A squeak stuns rivals, and a stunned bat hangs still in the air: it can't fly, dash or parry, so stun-then-dash is the safe play. Two ways to win, picked in the Battle rules: **Free-for-all** (first to 3, 5 or 7 bites) or **Rounds** (a chomped bat is out for the round; the last bat flying wins the round, first to 3, 5 or 7 round wins; after 90 seconds an echo storm lights everyone up). Bats start in different corners. A **Big Brawl** of 5 to 8 bats plays on a roomier arena (about a third bigger), opens with an "8-BAT BRAWL" title over the countdown and a banner flashing every bat's colour, starts the extra bats at the middles of the edges, and squeezes the score pills into compact ones (score, name and echo pips) so all eight fit. Each cave has its own look: crystal (blue-violet stone and glowing crystals), lava (red-brown rock, glowing lava slabs in the floor, embers), moss (green) and ice (pale frosty stone, glinting floor, snow). On ice, bats are slippery: they keep sliding and turn wider, and bounce harder off walls. Pick the arena in the Rules panel (a little map of it shows next to the name; see **Battle maps** below): **Morphing** (the blocks slowly reshape from one stone cave to the next), **Morphing Xtreme** (the cave turns icy crystal, lava and moss in turn, each with its own rules), one of the three themed caves kept still all match (Frozen Grotto, Lava Hollow or Mossy Den), or **Open Sky** (no walls, just a starry night). Online, guests see the host's arena pick as it changes. Each device controls one bat: you are Mo, and you fill the other seats with CPU bats or with friends online, one device each. On a keyboard, fly with WASD or the arrow keys, squeak with F, Space or Enter, dash with G and jump with R.
  - **3D or 2D**: battles are drawn in 3D by default, with a camera that follows your bat so the cave fills the screen. Switch to the flat top-down view on the setup screen or from the pause menu.
  - **CPU levels**: Easy, Normal or Hard. CPU bats play fair: they only know where you are when sound reveals you, you squeak or dash nearby, or you're right next to them; otherwise they hunt from where they last noticed you.
  - **Music and sound**: every mode has its own upbeat jazz tune played by a full band (trumpet, saxes and trombone, piano, walking bass, guitar, vibes and drums) plus sounds for stuns, blocks, power-ups and the countdown, all synthesized in the browser. The ♪ button in the bottom-left corner turns sound on or off.
  - **How to play guide**: before a match a "How to play" panel shows every control with its button (fly, squeak and parry, BITE·DASH, JUMP and POWER, plus the keys on a keyboard) and waits for **Start!**. Offline, you tap Start! (or press Enter); online, the host does and guests see "Waiting for the host to start". In Rounds, a short version (the controls in a row and a **Next round!** button) comes up before every next round.
  - **Dash**: a quick burst of speed with a short cooldown, and the only way to bite. Flick your finger, press the BITE·DASH button, or use the dash key (G). While you hold a special power-up, a POWER button pops up above BITE·DASH, so you can still bite. Two bats dashing into each other bounce apart.
  - **Jump**: the JUMP button left of BITE·DASH (or R; a second player on the same keyboard uses M, the third O and the fourth numpad . or 9) hops your bat up for a moment, over cave walls and other bats. The jump meter holds 3 jumps: the button's rim is split into three segments, each jump uses one, and they refill one at a time (about 1.8 seconds each, while you're on the ground); three little pips under your bat show the meter while it refills. There's a short pause after landing before the next hop (press just before you land and it hops again right away). You can jump even while stunned: you still can't fly, dash or squeak, so you hop straight up on the spot. Squeak rings, fireballs and ice blasts pass underneath, and a rival who dashes in to bite you up there snaps on empty air: the full chomp plays, with a whoosh and a big "MISS!", and you're unharmed (you can't bite from up there either). Land on rock and you're nudged out to the nearest gap. In 3D the bat rises with a shadow under it; in 2D it grows bigger over a dashed shadow. CPU bats jump too, on the same meter: to dodge an echo, a fireball or a bite (sometimes even while stunned), or to hop a thin wall.
  - **Parry**: squeak just as a rival's squeak or dash reaches you. You shrug it off, they get stunned, and your squeak is free.
  - **Power-ups** appear in the dark every few seconds: Mega Screech (your next squeak is huge and stuns longer), Speed (6 seconds), Shield (blocks one stun) and Echo Frenzy (5 seconds of free, rapid squeaks). Special power-ups are held until you use them: use them with the POWER button that appears above BITE·DASH (or press E or Q on a keyboard). Fireball, Freeze and Tornado (pulls rivals in) last 8 seconds from your first use (a ring on the button shows the time left). Fireballs and tornadoes fire as fast as you can tap; Freeze, which hits everything around you, needs a short moment between uses. Thunder (one big strike from the sky on every rival near you) and Ghost (fly through walls for 5 seconds) are single use. In the lobby's Rules panel, Power-ups opens a menu of every power-up (tap one to turn it off or on; the list scrolls with a slim glowing scroll bar you can drag, or swipe the list) and how often they come: Off, Low, Normal, High or **Extreme**. On High two show up the moment a round starts, then a new one every 2 to 3.5 seconds, up to 5 at once; on Extreme three are there at the start, then a flood of them every second or so, up to 8 at once. Online, guests see the host's choices.
- **Co-op Run** (Multiplayer → Co-op Run): 1 to 4 bats play together on one of two maps (the Map choice in Rules): **Side-scroll**, a long dark level whose screen keeps moving, or **Explore**, branching caves the team explores freely with lanterns as checkpoints (more in the Explore bullet below). Either way they fly while spiders, owls, cave crawlers and ghost moths hunt them. Squeak to see (an echo stuns nothing); the dive bite (dash) knocks out any monster it hits and the BITE button (X or H) swats everything in an arc in front, cutting spider threads and swatting crystal pieces too. Some ceiling crystals are loose: an echo cracks one and it bursts into sharp pieces that hurt bats and knock out monsters, and dashing through a spider's thread drops the spider to the floor. Each bat has 3 hearts; knocked-out bats come back when a teammate stays close to them for a few seconds (a ring fills up around the fallen bat) or reaches the next lantern (while yours is down, your ghost drifts over to the teammate it's watching and the camera follows a teammate who's still flying, with a "Watching: name" label; tap the screen, or press Space or an arrow key, to switch to another teammate), and if everyone goes down the team restarts from the last lantern (3 tries). Add CPU buddies or play with friends online. CPU buddies are helpers, not carries: they react late, sometimes fumble a slash or a bite, set off crystals overhead and get knocked out now and then, so on Side-scroll a team of only CPUs rarely gets out (on Explore they slowly hunt down keys and switches on their own). The Monsters level sets how tough the monsters are, and each buddy plays at its own seat's level. The camera stays close around your own bat (8 tiles tall), and arrows at the screen edge point to teammates out of view. Three ways to play:
  - **Classic**: the run above.
  - **Escape**: monsters can't be beaten. A bite, a slash or a crystal piece only knocks them back and dazes them for a moment, extra ghost moths and owls keep chasing the team, and the screen scrolls a little faster.
  - **Explore map**: Classic and Escape are a run of three caves (122×52 tiles each): the **Crystal Cave**, then the **Lava Cave** (glowing lava pools, drifting embers) and the **Ice Cave** (snowfall, and slippery ice like Battle's ice arena: bats keep sliding after they let go). Each cave gets its own look in 3D and 2D, a title card and a short countdown. The exit is a big cave mouth that stays barred until the team finds the cave's keys (1 in the first cave, 2 in the lava cave, 3 in the ice cave). The bottom bar counts keys and team hearts, and once every key is found the pointer leads to the mouth (before that it points to the next lantern, then to the nearest key). Some side chambers are sealed by glowing gates: fly into (or bite) the gate's lever switch and the bars slide up. Each switch is tucked away in its own small side cavern off a tunnel, round a corner and never on the way between the start, lanterns, keys and exit, and it stays dark until someone sees it (fly close or squeak near its cavern); the cave maker checks that no switch is ever shut behind a gate. Keys, Battle's power-ups (speed, shield, mega echo, frenzy, fireball, freeze, ghost) and hearts wait behind gates, at the ends of long side tunnels and in narrow crawls. A fireball, freeze or ghost is held: a POWER button appears above DASH while you hold one (or press E/Q), and a held fireball or freeze lasts 8 seconds from the first use. Hearts go into a shared team bag (up to 5): stay near a fallen teammate and after a moment one heart brings them back with 2 hearts (with the bag empty it takes a few seconds longer and they come back with 1). Lanterns still wake everyone, and if everyone goes down the team restarts from the last lantern. Flying out through the open mouth plays a fly-off: the bats burst out into a starry night, and after the last cave they loop around the moon and away to the stars before the results. The **minimap** in the bottom-right corner shows the team's explored cave around your bat; tap it (or press M or Tab; Tab when several players share a keyboard, since M is player 2's POWER key) for the big map of the cave: everything any bat has flown past or lit, shared with everyone online. Your bat blinks, teammates show in their colours, and once seen it marks the lanterns (lit or not), the cave mouth, keys, switches and their gates, hearts and power-ups (taken ones disappear). The run keeps going while it's open, so the map is see-through and its edge flashes red if you get hurt; tap anywhere to close it. Side-scroll has no map. CPU buddies go for keys, switches, hearts and power-ups, fly over to revive fallen teammates and use fireball and freeze on monsters. Hunt on the Explore map stays a single bigger cave (146×52) with gates and power-ups but no keys; reaching the open mouth ends it with the full fly-off.
  - **Hunt**: smash monsters for points. New monsters arrive in waves, quick kills in a row build a combo (up to ×5), and the run ends when the 2:30 clock runs out or someone reaches the green light (seconds left become bonus points). Push against the right edge to make the screen scroll faster. The end screen shows the team score and the best hunter.
- **Online** (in the Multiplayer lobby): the bottom row has **Create room** and a code field with its own Join button. One player taps Create room and shares the 4-letter code (the code tiles, a copy button and Invite replace the row) or the copied link; the others type the code (or open the link). A Battle room holds up to 8 players and a Co-op Run room up to 4. A friend who joins a match of four or fewer takes a CPU bat's seat. The host picks the game, the rules and CPU bats for empty seats, and guests see every choice. If someone leaves mid-match, a CPU bat takes over. **Back to room** keeps everyone connected: it's on the end-of-match popup (next to Main menu and Rematch / Play again) and in the in-match menu (the pause button; online the match keeps going while it's open). When the host picks it, the match ends and everyone goes back to the room's lobby with their seats. When a guest picks it, only they step out: mid-match a CPU flies their bat until the match ends, and their lobby says what they're waiting for; the host's next start (or rematch) brings them back in. Main menu (end popup) and Leave room (in-match menu) leave the room.
- **Voice chat** (online rooms, in the lobby and during Battle and Co-op matches): a mic button sits in the lobby's room row and, in a match, next to the ♪ button. Voice is opt-in: the first tap asks "Turn on voice chat?", and only then does the browser ask for the microphone. Three modes: **Off**, **Push to talk** (hold V, a key of your choice or a mouse side button, or hold the mic button) and **Open mic** (sends while your voice is over the line on the level meter). Computers start on Push to talk, phones and tablets on Open mic. A quick tap on the mic button opens the voice panel: mode, mute, the level meter with the open-mic line, talk key, microphone choice (when there is more than one), mic volume, noise filter / echo cancel / auto level, everyone's volume, and the people in the room with a glowing ring on whoever is talking, plus a volume slider and mute for each of them. The button glows green while you're on air; in a match, the names of whoever is talking show above it. Settings are saved on the device. Players' voices go directly between devices (WebRTC) when the network allows, and through the room server otherwise. Headphones help with open mic.


### Battle maps

- **Frozen Grotto**: mirrored crystals on slippery ice. Crystal islands mirrored in every quarter of the arena (so each start corner is fair) on a floor of solid ice: bats keep sliding after they let go, turn wider and bounce harder off the rock. (It replaces Crystal Grotto and Frozen Cavern; an old saved Frozen Cavern pick opens Frozen Grotto.)
- **Lava Hollow**: the floor is lava. Rounded, organic rock islands (one big island in the middle, smaller ones around it) stand in it, their edges glowing where the lava laps at them. Stay on top and hop from island to island (JUMP); a bat that lands in the lava sizzles and is knocked out (in Free-for-all, the last rival that hit it in the few seconds before gets the point).
- **Mossy Den**: the blocks are bushes on grass. Dash into one to chomp right through it; it grows back after a while.
- Standing on a block top (crystal, bush, rock or plain wall) is sticky: drifting, ice momentum or a small bump won't carry you off. Push out past the edge for a moment (or dash off it) to step down, and the bat does a little hop down to the floor with a squash and a puff of dust or frost when it lands. A big hit can still knock you off.
- **Morphing** reshapes between plain stone caves; **Morphing Xtreme** holds each of the three maps above for a while, then reshapes quickly into the next (Frozen Grotto, Lava Hollow, Mossy Den), with a 3-2-1 warning before the floor turns to lava.
- Power-ups: Mega Screech, Speed, Shield, Echo Frenzy, Fireball, Thunder, Freeze, Tornado and Ghost (Stone Wall is gone), from Off up to Extreme.
- When you're out (Rounds, or waiting to come back), tap the screen to watch another bat still flying. The How to play guide before a match is a compact card.

### Controls

| | Phone / tablet | Computer |
|---|---|---|
| Fly | Drag anywhere | Arrow keys or WASD, or drag with the mouse |
| Squeak | Tap, or tap with a second finger while dragging | Space, or click |
| Map (Explore) | Tap the minimap, bottom right | M or Tab |

The game is designed for phones held sideways and also runs in any desktop browser.

### Running it

There is no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder with any static file server:

```sh
npx serve .
```

### Layout

- `index.html` contains the page, menus and meta tags for adding to the home screen.
- `src/game.js` contains the game loop, input, hazards and rendering on a single canvas.
- `src/level.js` contains the Explore cave maps, drawn as text with one character per tile (the key is at the top of the file), and the Cave Run tunnel generator.
- `src/arenas.js` contains the Bat Brawl arena maps and their color themes.
- `src/duel.js` contains Bat Brawl: stun and eat rules, the eating animation, arena modes and Last Bat Standing rounds, touch controls, the CPU bats and the 2D view.
- `src/view3d.js` draws the game in 3D with three.js: Bat Brawl with a chase camera, and Explore and Cave Run as a cave cross-section with depth. The rules stay in `duel.js` and `game.js`.
- `src/coop.js` is Co-op Run (its levels come from `makeCoopLevel` in `src/level.js`).
- `src/net.js` contains online rooms: room codes, the lobby, and the connection between players.
- `src/voice.js` is voice chat for online rooms: the mic button and voice panel, push to talk and open mic, WebRTC voice between players and the room-server fallback.
- `server/rooms.js` and `wrangler.jsonc` are the Cloudflare Worker that serves the game and runs online rooms.
- `src/vendor/peerjs.min.js` is PeerJS 1.5.5 (MIT), loaded only when you open Online.
- `src/vendor/three.min.js` is three.js r158 (MIT), used for the 3D battle view.
- `src/chomp.js` draws the big chomp (jaws, teeth and slashes) every mode uses for bites and slashes.
- `src/looks.js` is the bat creator and bat looks: every part, 2D and 3D bat drawing, and the editor.
- `src/backdrop.js` paints the cave behind the menus, and `src/cardart.js` paints the illustrated menu cards.
- `src/music.js` is the synthesized jazz band: four 32-bar tunes, one per mode.
- `src/style.css` styles the menus and overlays.

### How online works

The host's browser runs the match and sends snapshots to everyone about 20 times a second; guests send their stick and button presses back. When the game runs on Cloudflare (see below), messages go through a small room server, which works on any network. Otherwise players connect directly over WebRTC using [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com/), and the free public PeerJS server is only used to find each other by room code. A few strict networks (some school or office Wi‑Fi) block direct connections; switching to mobile data usually fixes that.

Voice chat (`src/voice.js`) sends its signalling (who has voice on, WebRTC offers) through the room like game messages, then connects everyone with voice on directly (WebRTC audio, public STUN only). While two players have no direct link (many phone networks block them, and there is no TURN server), the talker also streams their voice through the room: 16 kHz mono, 4-bit ADPCM, 60 ms frames of 486 bytes (about 65 kbit/s), only while actually talking. The room server copies those binary frames to everyone who has voice on, at most 30 a second per sender, and skips anyone whose connection is backed up, so voice never holds up the game. With `?net=local`, test tabs pass voice over their own BroadcastChannel; on PeerJS it goes through the host. For testing, `?voice=relay` skips WebRTC and `?voice=rtc` never uses the room relay.

### Cloudflare

The repo deploys as one Cloudflare Worker: it serves the game and gives each room code a Durable Object that relays messages between players. In the Cloudflare dashboard, go to Workers & Pages, choose Create, then Import a repository, and pick this repo (deploy command `npx wrangler deploy`). The game then lives at `https://mocaka.<your-subdomain>.workers.dev` and uses its own room server automatically. Copies hosted elsewhere use it once `ROOM_SERVER` in `src/net.js` is set to that address; `?relay=<address>` does the same for one visit. Locally, `npx wrangler dev` runs the whole thing.

Online needs the game served from a real web address (for example Netlify or GitHub Pages); it does not work from a local file. To try it on one computer, open two tabs with `?net=local`, which connects tabs through the browser instead of the network.
